| Environment and materials | Rows of similarly sized firs share the same silhouettes. Water, gravel, wood and moss read mostly as flat colors with repeated marks. | REGOLITH uses irregular relief, believable scale and contingent marks on terrain. The v6 scenes distinguish masonry, glass and vegetation at a glance. Establish wet cedar grain, broken end grain, striated rock, saturated moss and shallows in authored assets. |
| Light | The moon and window are circles and rectangles over evenly lit scenery. The figures have little connection to the ground. | Zig separates practical lights from cool environment fill. v6 uses lamps and reflections to establish location and time. Use a directional warm break in the storm, cool forest bounce, selective window glow and consistent actor contact shadows. |
| Composition and spatial depth | Near and far forests have similar silhouettes and density. The UI interrupts the actors and most important damage. | REGOLITH establishes scale with graded silhouette sizes and perspective. The v6 view frames a location with foreground foliage. Use a visible near-bank trail, a dark foreground frame with occlusion, readable river surface and progressively lower-contrast forest ridges. |
| Character and props | Primitive bodies give the ranger and player few distinguishing traits. The evidence overlays use line diagrams that are visually detached from the photographed world target. | Paper Town’s Godot source separates character roles and uses depth-of-field and fog as scene layers. The chosen character frame is stored in Git LFS and its media URL returned HTTP 403, so a direct frame comparison was unavailable. Its published materials were used for reference only. Use readable silhouettes at about 8–11% frame height: ochre coat and brim for Lin, teal anorak and burgundy cap for the scout. Match inspected evidence to the same rendered props. |
| Visual storytelling | The otherwise complete-looking bridge has a tiny omission hidden by a full-width label. The safe crossing is too close tonally to the unsafe water. | Show a large translucent gap with jagged ends and intact support beams. ## Production brief

A grounded, richly painted Pacific Northwest river after a storm. Slate-teal water, cedar-green midtones, warm cream late light, rust and amber as story accents. Fixed elevated oblique viewpoint. One connected location. Damp gravel leads from the left shelter to the damaged span and down to the right ford. Fog separates the ridges. Locked geometry keeps the broken and repaired silhouette legible at the same place and scale.

Start with a production plate, a two-state bridge and a character/prop contact sheet. Keep original outputs and prompts privately available for rework. Register each optimized asset with origin, edits, rights scope and SHA-256. Key transparent sprites and crop with a reusable pipeline. Use a texture atlas with consistent feet anchors for idle, opposing walk strides and gestures. Scene anchors must be verified in the real mobile crop and at the far bank. Production build embeds optimized art locally, no runtime fetches.

Review sequence: establishing plate and on-screen actors at desktop/phone; bridge silhouette; interactive ranger/evidence frames; both outcomes; compact/reduced-motion. A visually weak result returns to asset production before widening scope.
