## World and route

At dusk a ferry ties up at a busy terminal. Its brass departure board reads EMPTY CROSSING. Nobody boards. Mina, the sign painter, can read the old dockworkers’ term “ghost run”; Sol, the dispatcher, controls the new board. Each thinks the other ordered the service withdrawn. The deck tally proves there were passengers. Discover why a dictionary installed to make announcements easier for outsiders has erased a working route. The mechanism applied one current citywide definition to every decade and every speaker. Nobody secretly cancelled the boat.

Walk from the concourse to the sign loft by the stairs or to the crew shelter along the quay. Both return to the board; from there the raised gangway leads to the ferry’s public-address booth. A working ticket press in the loft accepts two dated strips and one audience plate. Register the strips against physical tick marks, inspect a punched ticket and a crew note, and set the destination visible to the reader. The early strip uses “ghost run” for an empty return. A later strip plus a ticket punched after the last scheduled sailing shows the crew using it for an unlisted pickup. Both senses can be true. A repost or a ticket alone cannot establish common usage. Created terms, machinery, places, dialogues and historical evidence are fictional.

Slice: concourse and adjoining loft, with one continuous walkable threshold, the press, Mina and a return to the platform. Ask what the word used to mean before Mina opens her dated drawer. Supply a now/then explanation plus evidence to authorize a reversible test print. Roll the sign to its chosen wording; show the queue split or reunite visibly, preserve the changed sign on revisit. The wrong date or unsupported gloss prints the wrong destination on a proof: compare the misrouted test ticket with the docket, rewind and retry without losing evidence. Slice runtime estimate 12–15 minutes. Full game estimate 45–55 minutes of investigation, plus 20–25 minutes of writing, oral briefing and exchange. Longer writing may span two sessions.

Full branches: publish crew wording with a gloss and Mina opens the shelter archive; an unfamiliar visitor still needs a plain boarding instruction. Lead with a plain public notice and Sol opens the dispatcher logs; Mina asks for the local term to be preserved on the quay. Each route supplies equivalent evidence through a different witness and a different physical task. Both return to the gangway. Final ruling can retain both labels or assign each to its audience. The ferry sails once passengers can act on the notice. End on the same board and the opening question. Preserve evidence provenance, inferred senses, witness trust, print proof, board text, queue arrangement, route, drafts, support use and oral observation separately. A dissenting argument about Internet language remains valid on both routes.

### Supports and assessment

Point-of-use glosses: usage 用法, accepted 被认可的, standard 标准的, slang 俚语, degrade 使退化, ban 禁止, rapidly 迅速地, strengthen 加强, convey 传达, noticeable 明显的, fascinated 着迷的, bothered 感到困扰的, authority 决定权, robust 有活力的. Optional task language: crew 船员, crossing 渡运班次, docket 调度单, register 对齐 (press alignment). Enrichment: semantic shift 词义变化, register 语域 (linguistic sense). Avoid imposing these extensions as assessed vocabulary.

CORE: landmarks, short replayable turns, paired roles, two evidence pieces at a time, sentence starters and private rehearsal. Keep the full argument; draft it in chunks. STRETCH: include contradictory testimony and a repeated source; invite an unsolicited counterexample, reconcile both senses and revise an overbroad claim. Allow switching supports without restarting. Likely barriers: interpreting informal as incorrect, treating dictionary entry or viral repetition as proof of acceptance, confusing “did … use to” with “used to”, copying a definition without testing a recipient's understanding. Hint order: draw attention to the passenger or date, compare the two objects, only then reveal a frame. Never score a viewpoint. Offline checks enforce evidence IDs, route logic and submitted output; teacher judges grammar, reasoning, listening mode and oral performance.
