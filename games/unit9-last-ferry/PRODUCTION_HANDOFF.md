## Teacher-only story truth
An evil wizard cast a curse that makes the school forget it is a school. The castle, assigned roles and lost memories are effects. Slang is ordinary community language and clues reveal its changing meanings. Keep the culprit and spell out of student-facing titles, hints, dictionary entries, chapter labels and early narration. First bell: familiar school spaces change into a castle. Second: the lunch server forgets the students she knew. Third: the royal register fixes each person's role. Advance bells per player at story milestones; waiting, replay and reading carry no time penalty. Plant a bell-rope seal and a repeated correction in the register as early clues that could plausibly be school or royal marks. At the ending the player aligns the marks with the wizard's signed binding order. Reveal his authorship and purpose: a school that forgets itself becomes a court whose members know only their assigned roles. Let the student use evidence and testimony to challenge the reading and break the curse. Show the restored school and the effects of the student's wording on how Mei is remembered. Both defensible audience choices can succeed.

## Solo slice and GM contract
Route: familiar classroom door → transformed council landing → empty chair and dated dictionary → feast-hall witness → return to council. Physical clue: a worn seat reservation underneath the chair carries the same phrase as the prefect's request. Let students lift the tag, compare it with the entry, listen/replay and question a witness. “Save a shadow” used to mean keeping a seat; modern speakers can use it to mean speaking on someone's behalf. Keeping the chair is a reasonable, recoverable first interpretation. Mei is present and has an account of her own. Students can help her speak for herself or convey her testimony with her consent. The guard moves, Mei takes the floor, and the path to the hall opens; retain the result on revisit and reload. End the slice on the second bell. The full-game reveal belongs later.

Each student plays alone under a session alias. Never require another student for evidence, oral work or continuation. ## WIDA 3 language design across all surfaces
Target approximately WIDA level 3 for secondary learners. This is a design target, not a student score. Use direct contemporary English in spoken lines, choices, instructions, captions, evidence, definitions, hints, sponge, GM lines addressed to students and prompts. Preserve the medieval setting through art and motive. Give short coherent passages, concrete referents, explicit time cues, one actionable instruction at a time, and chances to link ideas with but, because and so. Teach essential academic words in context with optional Chinese glosses and audio replay. No assumed knowledge of invented slang. CORE: picture + old/new examples + optional frame. STRETCH: same accessible language, an additional witness or alternative inference. Frame: “It used to mean ___. Now it can mean ___. I think so because ___.” Fade the frame on a second example. Teacher-only controls can use professional language. Audit receptively and expressively: listen for meaning/attitude, read a clue, explain a change aloud, write linked reasons. Make supported multi-paragraph writing and a one-minute presentation possible. No claim of official WIDA scoring.
