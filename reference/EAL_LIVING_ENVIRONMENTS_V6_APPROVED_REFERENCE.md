## Required patterns for new game production

- **Scene-specific cinematic ambience:** animate elements motivated by the physical scene (lamps, wet reflections, screens, foliage, drifting atmosphere), never a repetitive whole-image pan or generic overlay.
- **Readability first:** scenery and clue objects remain legible; structural elements remain stationary unless story actions change them. Motion is subtle, does not impose reading time pressure, and can be switched off.
- **Focus View:** a presentation-oriented environment view with side panels hidden, while a clear path back to interaction is retained.
- **Offline-first:** self-contained HTML or fully local package; no external JS, fonts, images, APIs, network requests, or VPN required.
- **Responsive UI:** usable desktop and phone layouts, touch-accessible controls, and an explicit mobile-width QA check.
- **Accessibility:** respect reduced-motion preferences by default, allow explicit motion override where appropriate, and never place required evidence only in animation/audio.
- **Curriculum fidelity:** original unit screenshots, vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, Chinese glosses, and differentiated EAL supports govern every new game; use language to cause meaningful in-world action.
- **World depth and puzzle variety:** the v6 builds establish presentation/technical baselines only. Future games must still pursue more original, diegetic puzzle mechanics, tangible evidence objects, persistent before/after world changes, distinct environments, and consequential player decisions, as required by the Next-Generation Standard.
- **Honest QA:** validate actual interaction paths and save/report export; document exactly what passed and what has not been tested. No claiming physical iPhone Safari verification from simulated browser tests.

## Boundary: these v6 builds are not a ceiling

These files use restrained **procedural HTML/canvas ambience over still photos**; they do not contain fully animated cinematic footage. Some later-location photomontages and schematic scenes remain below the ultimate photographic-world standard. For new games, follow the higher visual requirements in the standing quality standard, using Far Horizon for cinematographic polish **without reusing its IP, images, art, or story**.

**Instruction for future tasks:** inspect these **exact two v6 files** and the quality brief before designing, revising, or approving any new immersive offline EAL game. Preserve functioning approved elements; raise quality through targeted improvements and benchmark against both full gameplay and presentation, not an image mockup.
